CaseTranspomobility start-up, Abidjan, 2026

Connecting drivers and fleets in a market without a register

Transpo is a start-up for car-rental fleets that work with ride-hailing apps in Abidjan. This case shows the discovery before the build: what fleets and drivers lose today, the rules we decided on, and the flows and architecture that follow from them.

01The market

Apps match passengers and drivers. Nobody matches drivers and cars.

In Abidjan, almost every ride-hailing car belongs to a fleet, and drivers rent them by the day. The link between passenger and driver is an app. The link between driver and fleet is a phone call, a chat and an office.

The insight

Both sides lose when a car changes hands. Today every handover is a negotiation without a record.

How might we

hand a car to a checked driver as easily as an app hands a ride to a passenger, and keep a fair record of who had it?

Context

For drivers who read little

Many fleet drivers read little and work on cheap phones in strong sun. So every screen speaks: Listen reads it aloud, including which button to press.

Layer 1, the flow

Two steps, no office

Find and book a car, then open it with a finger. The dispatcher sees the shift start in the fleet console.

Layer 2, wireframes

One big action per screen

Low-fi frames fix the order: Listen on top, the map, the nearest fleet, one button in reach of the thumb.

Layer 3, colour test

Black and white first

In direct sun a cheap screen loses most of its contrast. White on black holds 18:1.

Layer 3, colour test

Brand violet, too light outside

White on the brand violet gives 5.7:1: fine indoors, washed out in direct sun.

Layer 3, colour test

A darker violet for the street

White on #5b21b6 gives 9:1. Violet marks the voice and the nearest car park, black marks the action.

Layer 4, components

Built from a few parts

A Listen bar, a car park marker, status chips and one big action repeat on every driver screen.

08Next, in the field

Check the people, then check the screens

This is the first stage. The proto-personas and the screens go to Abidjan together: first we confirm who the drivers are, then we watch whether the screens work for them.

1. Turn proto-personas into personas

Interviews with drivers in each segment and with 3 to 5 fleets

  1. How well drivers read, and which languages they speak at workdecides how much the voice has to carry
  2. How a car changes hands today, step by stepthe moment the product is built around
  3. Prayer, Ramadan and the rhythm of a shiftopen in the studies
  4. Which proto-persona is real, which merges, which is missingsix drivers is a draft count

2. Test the screens with those drivers

Clickable frames on a cheap phone, outdoors, in the drivers’ own words

  1. Can a driver book and unlock with Listen alone, without reading?the core claim of the design
  2. Is the dark violet readable at midday on a scratched screen?the colour test above, checked in the street
  3. Is one big action enough, or do drivers look for more?task success and hesitation
  4. Does the voice sound like help, or like an order?tone and language of the script
Research the handover, not the appThe pain sits in the moment a car changes hands. The app is only the way to record it.
Fairness rules are product rulesWho can be blocked, when and for whose debt decided more of the design than any layout.
A simulation is a draft, not evidenceIt finds gaps in our own logic fast. Every finding stays a hypothesis until a real driver says it.
