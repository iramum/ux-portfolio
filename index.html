01WorkspaceB2B fintech, internal tool, 2026
Case study, B2B fintech, internal tool

Workspace

A workspace for deal managers, built on top of a company's internal console. The console holds every deal, document and payment. The workspace shows the manager only what needs them today, and one action for each problem.

Company
A private capital company that funds deals against property. Not named on request
My role
Product & UX Designer: research, flows, architecture, prototypes, UI
Worked with
CEO as product owner
Tools
Miro, FigJam, Figma, AI-assisted prototyping in code
Result
UX path, interactive prototype, first visual direction
141days the first deal took
68days the ideal process needs

1The task

One deal, nine stages, four parties

Each deal runs through a special-purpose company, an SPV. The manager aligns terms with the client, registers the SPV, passes KYC with an external corporate service provider (CSP), splits shares, signs contracts, registers the purchase, and then follows the monthly fee until the exit.

Four parties hand work to each other the whole time: the manager, the client, the CSP and the land registry. One missing document blocks the next stage. The company's console stored all of it, but it was built for the CEO: every field, every status, for every role. A manager had to dig for what to do next.

The brief

Design the place the deal manager opens every morning

One place that says what needs the manager today, where every deal stands, and whose move it is. The console stays the source of data. The workspace is the manager's view of it.

Scope of this case

Home, deal portfolio, deal room, documents

Designed in depth for stages 01 to 05, from a lead that passed Screening to signed contracts. Leads and Payments are reserved in the menu and were not designed.

Honest status

A design proposal

An interactive prototype and a first visual direction. The console was built in parallel, and the two were not merged.

2The problem

A spreadsheet of events, a board drawn after the fact

What research showed: the delay was never in the work. It was in the waiting.

I went through every event of the first deal with the CEO and tagged each one by who held the ball.

  1. «Where is it stuck?» had no answer. Status lived in people's heads, history on a board, documents in email.
  2. Nobody owns the wait. Four requests from the CSP in a row. Each restarted the clock, nobody saw the total.
  3. Stages overlap. KYC can start while the SPV is still being registered. A strict tracker hides it.
  4. The manager does not need the money side. Margins and capital providers are for the CEO and the CFO. The manager acts on documents, people and dates.
How might we show the manager, every morning, what is burning, where, and what to do now, without the rest of the console?

4The path

Every path from lead to exit, and the decisions it forced

  1. One exit from Leads. Leads answer «can we work with this case?», the deal answers «on what terms?». Three rounds: an NDA did not draw the line, documents did not, the question did.
  2. The real order of 01. The KYC list was a missing step. Sending it in 01 lets the 21 days of KYC start sooner.
  3. Whose move. Every open item has an owner: you, the client, the CSP, or the CEO and the CFO. One request for everything missing, reminders on day 1, 4 and 7.
  4. Two views, one record. The CEO thinks in statuses, the manager in steps. The console's statuses stay as quiet tags under the manager's steps.
  5. Hand over, do not decide. After the contracts the manager hands the deal over. The CEO allocates the capital.
  6. Two kinds of close. A lead closes as Declined or Void with a reason. A deal closes at any stage with the manager's own words.
5Iterations

From one deal to a whole working day

Every version was built in code, so the CEO clicked through real stage data in each review. Versions 1 to 5 tried to show one deal better: document lists, step cards, a chain, thin rows, a kanban. 6 and 7 asked what the manager looks at first. 8 and 9 grew the list into a workspace.

6Final design

Home, deal portfolio, deal room

  1. A tile per section. Payments and Leads are reserved, dashed.
  2. Plan your day. Filled by the system: what is late, silent, new to check or due today.
  3. Today or Later. The only choice the manager makes here.
  4. Pinned deals. Deals to keep an eye on.
  5. My notes. Not tasks. A note can become one with «Make a task».
  6. One board. To do, Doing, Done. Work done in a deal room lands in Done by itself.
  7. History. What was taken, moved and done, with the time.
  8. Own tasks. For what the system cannot know: a call, a question to the CFO.
  1. Urgent on the left. Late fees first. Coral only for what is late.
  2. The result on the right. Capital deployed, the one cobalt tile on the screen.
  3. Today. The three most urgent deals as cards: how late, which stage, whose move.
  4. One button, the action. Opens the deal room with the right panel already open.
  5. Also today. The rest in one line, so the list starts high.
  6. Ready to become a deal. Leads that passed Screening.
  7. Filters with counts. Same names and the same rule as the blocks above.
  8. Name the deals by. The client until funding, the SPV after.
  1. The deal and its team. Days over the ideal, each person's last actions.
  2. Facts, one click deep. Property, company and terms open as cards.
  3. What holds it. One line. Pink when the move is not ours.
  4. Stages, measured. Days against the ideal. Two stages can run at once.
  5. Next actions. Whose move, the task, the deadline, one button.
  6. Documents of the stage. What is missing goes straight into Next actions.
  7. Client contact. Next contact in the preferred channel. Calls are logged to the client card.
  8. Recent activity. Who did what, and what it means for the manager.

8Visual design and outcome

Air, one cobalt card, coral only for late

Rule 1

Cards without borders

White cards on a misty background, a soft shadow, a large radius.

Rule 2

One cobalt card per screen

The brand blue marks the result: capital deployed, work done.

Rule 3

Coral only for late

Late and missing get a coral outline and a pale pink fill.

Rule 4

A glow, fixed in the corner

It stays in place while the cards scroll over it.

Before

Delays seen after the deal closed

A spreadsheet of events, a board drawn after the fact, email and WhatsApp. One console card for every role.

After

One action per problem

A Home that plans the day, one list for all deals, a room per deal. Every stage measured against its ideal, every open item with an owner.

Next

What I would do next

Leads and Payments in the same workspace, empty and error states, a contrast check of the small grey labels, a moderated test with five or six managers.

Measure

What I would measure

Days per stage against the ideal, hours from «Screening passed» to «Start the deal», repeated document requests per deal.

What I learned: the step forward came from asking what the manager does first in the morning. The second came from separating two views of one record: the CEO thinks in statuses, the manager in next steps. Prototyping in code with real stage data let us throw away versions without anyone feeling it was wasted work.

Commonplace: a day with Nora

A workspace for writers who publish in several digital outlets. The case follows one writer through one day, from the first coffee to the next morning, and shows what the product does at each moment, and why.

Product
Commonplace, «Oops, I wrote it again.» Web app for phone and desktop
My role
Product and UX design, flow, information architecture, UI
Tools
Figma, AI-assisted coding (Claude Code)
Status
Working prototype on invented data

1The day

Nine moments, nine screens

  1. 07:30What needs me first? Today
  2. 08:15Why did it drop? Needs attention
  3. 13:10Write it down. Notes
  4. 16:00My head is empty. Ideas
  5. 16:15Help me start. Brief for AI
  6. 16:40Give it a date. Plan
  7. 18:00Send a bio. About me
  8. 22:40It slipped. Move it
  9. 07:00What still lives? Darlings

Phone to catch, desk to decide. Each screen answers one sentence the writer would say at one moment of the day; features that answered none of them stayed out. AI prepares the brief and checks facts, publishing and letting a piece go stay her call.

Behind the day: desk research on 34 sources, from the problems writers run into to what the product does about each of them, then the map of twelve screens and the flow from a note to a published piece.
